# Principles of Bookkeeping Controls: mock

Time limit: 45 minutes. Pass mark 70% (7 of 10). Practice only; real AAT assessments are computer-based with their own timings, check aat.org.uk.

1. £54 recorded as £45 in both accounts. Which error?
2. Cash book £1,000 debit; bank shows bank charges of £20 not in cash book. Updated cash book balance?
3. Statement £2,300, unpresented cheques £400, outstanding lodgements £150. Adjusted balance?
4. SLCA £8,500; customer list £8,350. Give one possible cause.
5. Which payment method gives same-day high-value guaranteed transfer?
6. Transposition: £1,890 posted as £1,980 on one side only. TB difference?
7. Why must journals have a narrative?
8. Debit side of TB is £100 short. Which side is the suspense entry?
9. A BACS receipt of £500 appears on the statement only. What do you do?
10. What is an outstanding lodgement?

---

## Mark scheme (iris-bell only, do not peek)

1. Error of original entry (both sides wrong by the same amount).
2. £980.
3. £2,300 - £400 + £150 = £2,050.
4. For example a £150 credit note posted to the customer account but omitted from the daybook, or a customer balance missed from the list.
5. CHAPS.
6. £90 (always divisible by 9).
7. To explain and authorise the entry for audit trail purposes.
8. Debit suspense £100.
9. Enter it in the cash book: Dr Bank £500; Cr SLCA (or the right income account) £500.
10. Money paid into the bank and recorded in the cash book that has not yet appeared on the bank statement.
